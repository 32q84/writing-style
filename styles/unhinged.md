# Unhinged

## Understand the comic relationship

Treat the reader as someone who can recognize a joke without having it explained. Keep something real underneath: an announcement, result, frustration, product change or observation. Create a mismatch between that substance and how the speaker presents it.

Let an ordinary event receive an absurd degree of ceremony, let a boast grow beyond sense, or let the speaker puncture their own promotion. The humor comes from the relationship between those parts, not from scattering slang, profanity and capital letters across normal copy.

## Choose a mechanism, not a costume

| Mechanism | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| Escalation and collapse | Start with a supplied achievement, escalate the speaker's rhetoric, then abruptly undercut their status or triumph. Keep real performance claims distinguishable from obvious absurdity. |
| Disproportionate ceremony | Give an ordinary event the language or gravity of a historic occasion. Let the mismatch remain visible. |
| Anti-promotion | Put an obviously self-defeating claim inside a recognizable promotional form. Make the context sufficient to read it as a joke rather than a plausible product limitation. |
| Sudden change of register | Allow a mostly ordinary explanation to take one unexpected turn. Do not announce that a punchline is coming. |

These are observed mechanisms, not a required sequence or an exhaustive list. Pick the one that suits the brief. Do not combine every mechanism in every post.

A reversal is not automatically a joke worth using. Do not stop at the most obvious inversion of a feature's benefit: [002-6](../calibration/002.md#002-6--unhinged) was rejected for being too obvious. Look for a surprising perspective on the subject, not merely a benefit phrased as a contradiction. Keep the language immediately understandable; making a predictable joke obscure or complicated does not improve the premise.

## Control the delivery

- Let the setup establish enough reality for the absurd turn to work. Sometimes a quoted post or image supplies the setup; do not assume a reader has that context if it will not accompany the copy.
- Deliver the absurd part with a straight face. Avoid a wink, an explanation, "just kidding" or a follow-up sentence explaining why the situation is funny.
- Use repetition, line breaks or capitals for timing and escalation. A quiet sentence can be just as absurd as an all-caps one.
- Keep the speaker involved in the joke. Boasting followed by self-deflation creates a different relationship from simply insulting the audience.
- Stop when the turn lands. Do not append a normal marketing slogan, summary or unrelated call to action that undoes the ending. Put necessary instructions earlier or in a separate reply when appropriate.
- Do not add a second joke merely to finish on a punchline. Let the absurdity arise from the subject, such as treating an ordinary technology as a new discovery. Mock honors and ceremonial sign-offs are not inherently clever; they need to earn their place in that specific premise. In [001-6](../calibration/001.md#001-6--unhinged), the user liked the opening's meta absurdity but rejected the ending; [001-5](../calibration/001.md#001-5--unhinged) was rated dull. Do not turn the approved opening into a fixed template or infer that every ceremonial ending is disallowed.
- Keep factual claims grounded in the brief. Do not invent benchmarks, endorsements or failures and defend them as comedy. Make exaggeration recognizably absurd rather than plausibly misleading.

## Keep it separate from the other styles

Use Builder when the main purpose is a candid explanation, even if it includes swearing. Use Brand when the main purpose is a structured product message. Here, construct a comic effect around the message. Do not treat "unhinged" as permission for random non sequiturs, hostility or a stack of current memes unrelated to the subject.

## Source examples

### Escalation, then self-deflation

Source excerpt: [Synoptic, SEC versus Binance](https://x.com/SynopticCom/status/1928204267942301977).

> FASTER THAN TWITTER
> FASTER THAN NEWS
> FASTER THAN INTERNET
>
> AS USUAL
>
> BUT NOBODY CARES

The preceding sentence supplies an event and timestamp. Notice how the comparison becomes absurd at "INTERNET," then the final line reverses the speaker's triumph. Preserve the escalation and reversal as a technique; do not turn "BUT NOBODY CARES" into a mandatory ending or treat this as verified speed evidence.

### Ceremony disproportionate to the occasion

Source excerpt: [Synoptic, scraping tournament](https://x.com/SynopticCom/status/1958975572954292684).

> Best of luck to everyone
>
> AND GOD BLESS THE UNITED STATES OF AMERICA

The surrounding post gives participant names and competition details. Keep enough ordinary material for the sudden ceremonial ending to clash with it. Do not reduce the lesson to "add an unrelated capitalized sentence."

### A promotional form contradicting itself

Source excerpt: [Summers, newswire launch](https://x.com/SummersThings/status/1950248108266573980).

> Our team is happy to announce that we have created SYNOPTIC NEWSWIRE, THE SLOWEST MARKET MOVING NEWS.
>
> This is an experimental feed so expect changes along the way.

Read this against the accounts' recurring promotion of speed. The conventional announcement carries an inverted pitch, then returns to ordinary product language without explaining the inversion. Use the shared context, not merely the word "slowest." Without that context, choose a joke that cannot be mistaken for a genuine limitation.

## Check the draft

Identify the real subject, the comic mismatch and the point where it lands. If there is no mismatch without the caps and swearing, change the idea rather than adding louder wording. If the joke obscures a necessary fact or creates a plausible false claim, rewrite it.
