# Builder

## Understand the relationship

Write as someone who built, investigated or uses the thing, talking to another person who might use it. Earn attention through direct knowledge: what was awkward, what changed, how it works and where it is useful. Let the reader see the reasoning rather than receive a polished set of selling points.

Assume the reader can understand the work. Explain unfamiliar mechanics without giving a lecture on concepts they already know. Use examples to make an action imaginable, not to decorate a claim about usefulness.

## Make the substance carry the voice

- Begin where the person's reason for speaking begins: a problem encountered, something shipped, a result noticed or a recommendation. Do not invent a struggle, personal experience or development history the brief does not supply.
- Make the subject identifiable in the opening sentence. A conversational setup should not make the reader work through a hypothetical situation before learning what changed. Compare the verdicts on [001-1](../calibration/001.md#001-1--builder) and [001-2](../calibration/001.md#001-2--builder); this is about immediate understanding, not a ban on problem-first openings.
- Explain cause and effect. If a feature removes a step, name that step. If a reader supplies an input and gets an output, show that exchange. Let the usefulness follow from the mechanism.
- Use a first-person observation or reaction when the speaker can truthfully own it. Company-wide facts can still use "we"; do not force every post into a personal anecdote.
- Allow a spoken sequence: explain one thing, add a necessary detail, offer an example. Use transitions such as "so" or "also" when the thought needs them, not as a checklist of voice markers.
- Keep distinctions, uncertainty and limitations that an operator would care about. Confidence can coexist with "happy to compare" or an explicit unknown.
- Finish with a relevant next step or invitation when helpful. A direct offer to answer questions fits; a generic engagement question does not.

Choose length according to what needs explaining. A personal reaction can be one line; a feature explanation may need several paragraphs. Lowercase, slang and swearing are optional surface choices, not requirements. Preserve spontaneity without manufacturing mistakes.

## Keep it separate from the other styles

Use this style to help the reader understand or act, even when the speaker is enthusiastic or irreverent. Do not turn every explanation into a slogan, which moves toward Brand, or build toward a punchline, which moves toward Unhinged. A swear word alone does not make something Unhinged.

## Source examples

### Explain the mechanism through a specific use

Source excerpt: [Synoptic, Custom Streams](https://x.com/SynopticCom/status/1976309244379955229).

> now, wtf are custom streams? think marketplace for scrapers.

> you set the targets on the seller’s scraper. say you want to track coinbase, cz, elon, and some memecoin influencers. you input their usernames, you pay per account and thats it - you get a normal stream. add it to the synoptic streamdeck dashboard or consume it on a webscoket.

Use the input, payment unit, output and delivery options to explain the product. Borrow that concreteness, not the typo or profanity. The surrounding post explains why the feature was needed and invites both users and vendors to participate.

### Make a claim with something the reader can inspect

Source excerpt: [Synoptic, CPI delivery](https://x.com/SynopticCom/status/1910328522029371871).

> Fastest CPI on Twitter at 8:30:01.368 ET. And you can access it through our API (Economic Indicators stream by OpticFeeds)
>
> Pretty sure its faster than big tradfi providers (if anyone has timestamps, happy to compare)

Notice the timestamp, named feed and invitation to compare. Preserve the distinction between the stated observation and the qualified comparison. This is a reference for the writing mechanism, not independent verification of the performance claim.

### Let a personal reaction remain personal

Source: [Summers, a customer using the platform](https://x.com/SummersThings/status/1983931535569907954).

> thats kind of crazy this huge prop firm is using our platform, feeling proud today

Keep the speaker's reaction when that is the point. Do not automatically convert it into a customer-success announcement or a lesson for the audience.

## Check the draft

Could this be said by the person doing the work? Does it explain something specific or express a genuine supplied reaction? If removing slang makes the whole voice disappear, develop the substance instead of adding more slang.
