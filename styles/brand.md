# Brand

## Understand the relationship

Write as the organization presenting something the reader should understand, evaluate or use. Let the product and its consequences take precedence over the writer's experience of building it. Make the information easy to follow without relying on the reader knowing the person behind the account.

Earn confidence through precise scope, clear definitions and supported benefits. Do not substitute a professional-sounding vocabulary for information. This voice can be direct, warm or assertive without becoming corporate ceremony.

## Organize around the reader's decision

- Identify what the reader needs first: what launched, what a product does, who a capability helps or how it differs from something they already know. Lead with that, not the company's excitement about announcing it.
- Order the remaining information by what the reader needs to understand or do next. Explain unfamiliar mechanics before asserting their consequences.
- State benefits through supported changes to the reader's work. Avoid attaching an adjective to the product and calling that a benefit.
- Use a precise distinction when it prevents a real misunderstanding. Do not manufacture a "not X, but Y" identity statement just to sound memorable.
- Write as "we," the named product or an impersonal announcement according to the brief. Avoid slipping into a founder's private reaction unless requested.
- Use an action that follows from the message: enable the setting, choose a feed, read the documentation. Omit it if the piece is complete without one.

Control pacing, but do not impose a house template on every post. A brief announcement, a feature definition and a technical launch can all use this voice. Short copy should still communicate something; long copy should earn its length through additional information.

## Keep it separate from the other styles

Remove backstage commentary unless it matters to the reader's decision. That distinguishes this from Builder. Keep jokes subordinate to the message; do not turn the announcement into a comic performance. That distinguishes this from Unhinged. Brand does not mean humorless, adjective-heavy or full of slogans.

## Source examples

### Correct an actual category mistake

Source excerpt: [Synoptic, Custom Streams](https://x.com/SynopticCom/status/1995922846875283747).

> Custom streams aren't watchlists or filters.
>
> They’re dedicated, custom, private feeds targeting accounts, sites, tickers and keywords that you want sourced by vendor scraping infrastructure.
>
> Example: track all new posts from a set of crypto founders, exchanges, and analysts.

Use the distinction because sourcing a feed and filtering an existing feed are materially different. Follow the definition with a use case. Do not generalize the opening into a requirement to begin every piece with a negation.

### Explain the user's workflow

Source excerpt: [Synoptic, API](https://x.com/SynopticCom/status/1998044272747323600).

> Synoptic gives systematic traders and teams one clean API. You don’t need to stitch together multiple vendors. Plug in once, pick the info you need, and receive events over websocket into bots, risk systems, dashboards, or AI agents.

Carry forward the integration sequence and named destinations. The phrase "one clean API" is not the useful mechanism by itself; the following sentences explain what the integration changes.

### Scale up to a technical announcement

Source excerpt: [Synoptic, Terminal launch](https://x.com/SynopticCom/status/2112544868103016900).

> Synoptic Terminal is live.
>
> Trade news in one click on Polymarket, Kalshi, Hyperliquid, Binance and Bybit.

The full post goes from the launch to matching mechanics, trading interaction and available sources. Use that progression when the reader needs those details; do not copy its product claims into another brief or assume that every launch needs this much detail.

## Check the draft

Can someone unfamiliar with the writer say what changed, why it matters to their task and what to do if interested? Remove polished sentences that fail to answer any of those questions. Do not add a slogan merely because the ending feels quiet.
