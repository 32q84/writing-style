# Shared writing rules

Apply these rules to newly generated copy in every style. Read the selected guide under `styles/` for the voice and its source examples. Follow the calibration procedure in [README](README.md) when generating or reviewing examples.

## Preserve meaning before adjusting voice

- Use the brief as the source of facts. Do not invent features, speed claims, metrics, prices, endorsements, customer stories, availability or urgency to make copy stronger.
- Keep qualifications that affect meaning. Do not turn a target into an achievement or an uncertain finding into a claim of fact.
- Let the current request override a style default, but flag any change that would require an unsupported factual claim. Do not silently resolve contradictory facts.
- Preserve source quotations and user-approved examples verbatim. The rules below govern new copy, not edits to the historical record.
- Keep fictional calibration facts and unreviewed drafts clearly labeled. They are not public product announcements or approved voice examples.

## Remove decoration, not information

- Do not use em dashes. Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses as the sentence requires. Do not replace every em dash with a spaced hyphen or double hyphen.
- Default to deleting adjectives and intensifiers that merely praise or amplify: powerful, seamless, robust, innovative, groundbreaking, revolutionary, cutting-edge, world-class, unparalleled, exciting, truly, incredibly. Replace the judgment with what the thing does, or omit it.
- Keep modifiers that distinguish facts: public versus private channels, paid versus free access, a failed request. Do not damage meaning to achieve an adjective count of zero.
- Avoid stock sales and AI-assistant vocabulary: unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, supercharge, delve, game-changer, next-level, transformative, leverage, harness, tapestry, realm, journey. Use a literal term when it is genuinely the subject, such as leverage in margin trading; do not swap a necessary term for an awkward synonym.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs. Replace "streamline your workflow" with the actual task and the step removed. Do not replace one vague promise with another.
- Prefer ordinary verbs: use, build, send, find, read, choose. Do not formalize them merely to make the writing sound professional.

## Remove prefabricated rhetoric

Avoid these constructions when they contribute no information:

| Habit | Edit |
| --- | --- |
| "In today's fast-paced / ever-evolving world..." | Start with the actual event, problem or claim. |
| "We're thrilled to announce..." / "Introducing the future of..." | Say what launched or changed. |
| "It's not just X. It's Y." / "More than a platform..." | State the relevant capability. Keep a contrast only if it corrects a real misunderstanding. |
| "Whether you're X, Y or Z..." / "From X to Y..." | Name the relevant reader or use case instead of manufacturing breadth. |
| "Imagine a world..." / "What if you could..." | Describe what the reader can actually do. |
| "Here's the thing." / "The best part?" / "Let's dive in." | Delete the signpost and deliver the point. |
| "The result?" followed by an adjective list | State the observed result or supported consequence. |
| Three vague benefits, three fragments or repeated contrasts | Keep only the points needed; do not force information into a rhythm. |
| "Ready to take X to the next level?" | Give a specific action if one is needed. Otherwise stop. |
| A closing summary, slogan or moral repeating the opening | End when the useful point or joke has landed. |

Do not mistake a literal word blacklist for a writing method. A sentence can avoid every listed word and still be generic. Check what it communicates and why a person would say it.

## Keep the delivery natural

- Do not force equal paragraph lengths, one-sentence paragraphs, symmetrical lists, rhetorical questions or a hook-benefit-CTA sequence into every piece. Choose structure for the information and the style.
- Use bullets for genuinely separate items or steps, not to pad a short point. Do not bold every claim or add decorative headings, emojis or hashtags by default.
- Do not add typos, fake hesitations, slang, profanity or lowercase lettering merely to simulate a human. Use the chosen style's reasoning and relationship to the reader.
- Do not sand away a personal reaction or an intentional comic exaggeration just because it is informal. In Unhinged, obvious absurdity can be the point; plausible product claims still require support. Stock announcement language can be used as a recognizable setup for parody, not as an excuse for generic marketing.
- Return the requested copy without explaining your choices unless asked. During calibration, provide IDs and the shared brief, but do not tell the user which candidate they should like.

## Edit before returning

Check facts and qualifiers first. Remove unsupported praise, stock constructions and sentences that only restate the point. Read for the chosen style's purpose, not for surface markers. Check punctuation last. These are editorial preferences, not a claim that any word or punctuation mark proves AI authorship.
